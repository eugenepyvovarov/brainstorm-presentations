How to Win the Largest Market in AI
How to Win the Largest Market in AI

From Sarah Wang / a16z on Etched.

Simple idea: when one job gets huge and predictable, we stop using general computers and build machines only for that job. That’s happening now for AI inference (running models for users).

1. The big idea Special jobs get special machines
1. The big idea Special jobs get special machines

Early on, nobody knows the shape of the work → buy flexible computers. Later, the work is huge and stable → custom hardware is faster and cheaper (like GPUs for graphics).

Flexible computers win when the future is unclear
Huge, stable jobs deserve dedicated hardware
We’ve seen this before (graphics, networking)
2. Training vs running AI Build once vs pay every use
2. Training vs running AI Build once vs pay every use

Training a model is a big one-time cost. Running it for every chat/agent call is ongoing — like film production vs every ticket sold. Usage is exploding.

Training = pay once to build the model
Inference = pay every time someone uses it
Usage is exploding (chat, agents, APIs)
3. Why today’s chips struggle GPUs are Swiss Army knives
3. Why today’s chips struggle GPUs are Swiss Army knives

GPUs can do almost anything, so they started the AI boom. But running a model is mostly shoveling data from memory, not fancy math. Tricks to go faster often make answers slower. Power (watts) is the real limit.

GPUs are general — great for exploration
Running AI is mostly memory traffic, not pure math
Going “faster overall” often means slower per user
Power budget matters more than raw chip flex
4. The market gap Big tech has custom chips; others don’t
4. The market gap Big tech has custom chips; others don’t

Google, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft already built custom AI hardware for themselves. You can sometimes rent it — hard to buy and put in your own data center. Everyone else still needs something designed for inference that they can own.

Hyperscalers already built private AI machines
Renting isn’t the same as owning your own fleet
Room for chips non-hyperscalers can actually buy
5. What Etched is doing Chips built only for running AI
5. What Etched is doing Chips built only for running AI

Startup building whole systems (chip → rack) for running AI, not training. Focus: more answers per watt of power, and linking chips so memory feels shared. Started early (2022); now shipping racks and standing up a large site.

Bet: running AI is the big market; GPUs aren’t the end
Goal: more AI answers per unit of power
Link chips so a rack acts like one big machine
Selling full systems, not just a lab demo chip
6. Takeaway Shipping at scale is the hard part
6. Takeaway Shipping at scale is the hard part

History says specialized hardware will exist. Few teams can design it and manufacture/ship it at real volume. That’s the moat a16z is underwriting with Etched.

Specialized AI hardware is inevitable
Hard part = building and shipping at scale
The whole machine is the product
